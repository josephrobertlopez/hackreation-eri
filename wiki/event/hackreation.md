## Paths: how you get a workspace

Teams have about six people (about 31 people expected, five teams). **There are at most five teams, and a team has at most eight people.** Extra people join a team with an open seat in an extra role (tester, writer, ERI expert), or work from their own Codespace or laptop and push a branch to the team fork. Past 40 people, Joey raises a team's cap on the day; there is no sixth team. ERI job coaches who bring the people they support count toward the 40 seats, and the fake-data and ask-before-you-photograph rules cover everyone. There are two primary doors and one backup.

| Path | Needs | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Codespace** (primary) | A free, verified GitHub account | Your own cloud workspace from `.devcontainer/`, opened **from the team fork**. The default is a **slim** image (Node 20, Python 3, git; no PHP, no Docker), enough for P1 to P4. Only for PHP, Docker or WordPress, create it with the **full** configuration instead ([how: START-HERE](../../START-HERE.md#path-1-codespaces-a-free-github-account-any-laptop-with-a-browser)); that image is the old, larger one. **Start times (single source).** Slim image: on Oct 30, 4 of 4 cold creates started at the same time were ready in about 76 seconds (a fifth create request did not start). So make an account and open one test Codespace on Friday night, and create Saturday's during kickoff. No door has been tested with a screen reader yet, so tell Joey what you find |

Team N opens `https://hack-teamN.taild76c1b.ts.net` (team 3 opens `hack-team3`). **The team model.** At kickoff the team picks a **captain** with a GitHub account. No device? No problem. Roles that need only paper, a phone or an iPad are in [START-HERE.md](../../START-HERE.md). Passwords are never in this repo: they are written on the team cards and handed out at team formation. Each team runs only its own editor, so teams never share a port.

## Judging

Judges: the ERI liaison and one or two developer judges. Joey hosts and does not score. **Rubric version: draft as of Thu Oct 29; Joey changes "draft" to "final" after the Fri Oct 30, 6 pm confirmation with the ERI liaison.** Any change after that is posted in the Meetup comments and here. Each judge scores only the rows they own, 0 to 5 per row (0 = nothing, 3 = works with gaps, 5 = we would use it). When two judges own a row, the row score is their average, to one decimal. Presentation is the mean of all judges' scores, to one decimal. A team's weighted total is the sum over the rows of (row score / 5) x weight, so the most it can be is 100. Worked example: ERI liaison scores 4 on Solves (24 points), 4 on Adopt (12 points); the developer judge 3 on Accessible (15 points), 4 on Works (16 points); Presentation mean 4.0 (8 points). Total 24 + 15 + 16 + 12 + 8 = 75.0. Tie order: higher "Solves ERI's problem", then higher "Works end to end", then Joey flips a coin. Joey prints a judge sheet with this table and score boxes. Judges never get a team password. If no developer judge comes, the ERI liaison scores their two rows too and also applies the README cap. If the liaison is absent, Joey names a stand-in before 2:30: another ERI staff member or a developer judge, never Joey. If GitHub is not reachable at the venue, the judge sees the README line in the team's demo on the venue laptop; Joey has it open.

| Criterion | Weight | Looks like | Scored by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Solves ERI's problem | 30% | An ERI staffer says "we'd use that" | ERI liaison |
| Accessible | 25% | Keyboard and screen reader pass ([checklist](../domain/accessibility.md)). The judge's evidence: the demo shows keyboard-only use, the team says which screen reader it used and what its tester found, and the judge may ask for one keyboard action live. With a recording, the clip and the team's tester report are the evidence. A team with no screen reader that did a keyboard pass and says so honestly still scores on the rest of the row | Developer judge |
