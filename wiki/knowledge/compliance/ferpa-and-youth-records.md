# FERPA and youth records

## TL;DR
- FERPA binds **schools**, not ERI directly. When a school discloses education records to ERI, though, FERPA's **redisclosure limit** (34 CFR 99.33) travels with them: ERI may use them only for the purpose they were given for, and may not pass them on without consent.
- IDEA sets a key rule for transition. Before a school releases personally identifiable information to an agency **providing or paying for transition services** (such as DVR or a vendor like ERI), it needs **consent from the parent, or from the student once they reach the age of majority** (34 CFR 300.622(b)(2)). The same consent is needed to invite that agency to the IEP meeting (300.321(b)(3)).
- In Wisconsin, rights **transfer to the student at 18** unless a court has found the student incompetent. The school notifies the student and parents (DPI form M-7). FERPA rights also transfer at 18 (34 CFR 99.5).
- Wisconsin pupil-records law (Wis. Stat. 118.125) makes all pupil records confidential and lets the person authorizing a release choose **which behavioral records** go out.

## Key facts
| Item | Rule | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| Education records | Records directly related to a student and kept by the school or someone acting for it. Excludes sole-possession notes, law enforcement unit records, and treatment records of students 18 or older | 34 CFR 99.3 |
| PII | Direct identifiers (name, family names, address, SSN, student ID) plus indirect ones (date and place of birth) and anything that identifies the student "with reasonable certainty" | 34 CFR 99.3 |
| Eligible student | 18 or older, or attending a postsecondary institution | 34 CFR 99.3 |
| Transfer of FERPA rights | Parents' rights pass to the eligible student | 34 CFR 99.5 |
| Written consent | Signed and dated. Specifies the records, purpose and recipients. Electronic consent is OK if it authenticates the signer | 34 CFR 99.30 |
| Redisclosure | Recipients may not redisclose without consent and may use the records only for the stated purpose | 34 CFR 99.33 |
| Disclosure log | Schools record each request and disclosure | 34 CFR 99.32 |
| Transition consent | Consent from the parent, or the student at the age of majority, before PII goes to agencies providing or paying for transition services | 34 CFR 300.622(b)(2) |
| IEP invitation | With consent, invite agencies likely to provide or pay for transition services | 34 CFR 300.321(b)(3) |
| Age-of-majority notice | The IEP must state, at least **1 year before** the age of majority, that the student was told about rights that will transfer | 34 CFR 300.320(c) |
| Wisconsin age of majority | 18, unless the student has been adjudicated incompetent. The LEA notifies student and parents (form M-7) | DPI M-7 guide; Wis. Stat. 115.807 **(verify text)** |
| Wisconsin pupil records | All confidential. Behavioral records (psych tests, evaluations, conversation records) vs. progress records (grades, attendance) | Wis. Stat. 118.125 |
| Adult pupil and parents | A school may share an adult pupil's records with a parent if the pupil is the parent's tax dependent, unless the pupil objects in writing | Wis. Stat. 118.125 |

## How it works
1. **Referral from a school.** A school may share IEP or evaluation data with ERI only with consent that names ERI (or a class of recipients), the records and the purpose. For transition services, that consent comes from the parent (student under 18) or from the student (18 or older, not adjudicated incompetent).
2. **Once ERI has the records.** They are no longer "education records" in ERI's hands. ERI is still bound by the redisclosure condition the school must attach. If DVR or a CLTS waiver agency also needs them, ERI needs a **new consent** or must have the school disclose directly.
3. **At 18.** The student signs their own consents from then on. Parents keep no automatic access to ERI's files. A guardian appointed by a court, or a supported decision-making agreement under Wis. Stat. ch. 52, changes who signs or who helps **(verify the specifics with counsel)**.
4. **ERI's own records** about youth (job coaching notes, DVR reports) are not FERPA records. They are governed by DVR rules (see [VR confidentiality](vr-confidentiality-34-cfr-361-38.md)), by HIPAA or 51.30 if clinical, and by ERI's contracts.

## Caveats and gotchas
- People often assume "the parent signed at 16, so we're fine." Consent given by a parent **before** the transfer of rights may need to be re-obtained from the student at 18. Ask counsel how to treat ongoing consents **(verify)**.
- The text of Wis. Stat. 115.807 could not be opened (the site blocked automated reading). The age-18 rule here comes from DPI's M-7 guide.
- University LMS and web contracts may involve FERPA "school official" status, where the contractor acts for the institution under 34 CFR 99.31(a)(1). That is a contract question for counsel, and different from the youth-services question.
- School-based health or psychological records may be covered by HIPAA or Wis. Stat. 146.81-84 rather than FERPA in some settings **(verify)**.
- **Questions for ERI's counsel/compliance:**
  1. Do ERI's school referral forms meet 34 CFR 99.30 and 300.622, and do they name every downstream recipient (DVR, county, CLTS)?
  2. How does ERI re-consent at the age of majority?
  3. Do ERI's contracts with universities make ERI a "school official" with FERPA duties?

## How ERI / a hackathon tool should use this
- **Hackathon:** synthetic students only. Do not upload real IEPs, even redacted ones, because indirect identifiers can re-identify a student "with reasonable certainty."
- **Production:**
  - Track `consent_signer` (parent, student or guardian), `student_dob` and an **age-18 alert** 90 days ahead to prompt re-consent.
  - Store the source and purpose limits of school records, and block forwarding them to recipients outside the consent.
  - Separate behavioral records from progress records.
- **A tool must NOT:** auto-share IEP content with employers or DVR without a matching consent. It must not keep sending parents information after 18 without the student's consent. It must not use IEP disability details in job-matching models without review (see [AI use policy](ai-use-policy-for-nonprofits.md)).
