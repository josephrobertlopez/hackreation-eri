# SSI work rules (income counting, exclusions, resources, reporting)

## TL;DR
- SSI is a needs-based cash benefit. In 2026 the federal benefit rate (FBR) is **$994/month** (individual) and **$1,491** (couple). SSA has announced **$1,029 / $1,544** for 2027, paid starting 2026-12-31.
- Work rarely makes SSI stop suddenly. After the $20 general and $65 earned income exclusions, SSI goes down **$1 for every $2** of remaining wages.
- The resource limit is **$2,000** for an individual and **$3,000** for a couple. ABLE balances up to $100,000 and PASS set-asides are excluded.
- Wisconsin adds a state supplement of **$92.16/month** for an individual (effective 2026-05-01, up from $83.78). Wages must be reported to SSA monthly.

## How it works
**Earned and unearned income.** Earned income is wages, net self-employment earnings, and sheltered workshop pay. Unearned income is everything else, such as SSDI, pensions, unemployment, and cash gifts. Unearned income lowers SSI almost dollar for dollar after the $20 exclusion.

**In-kind support and maintenance (ISM).** Free or reduced-cost shelter can lower SSI. Since 2024-09-30, food no longer counts as ISM. Shelter still does.

**Order of the earned income math (simplified):**
1. Subtract the Student Earned Income Exclusion, if eligible (applied first).
2. Subtract any unused part of the $20 general exclusion.
3. Subtract $65.
4. Subtract IRWE (impairment-related work expenses).
5. Divide the rest by 2.
6. Subtract BWE (blind work expenses) and PASS set-asides, if any.
7. The result is countable earned income. Add countable unearned income. Subtract the total from the FBR.

SSA's own example: $317 in wages, minus $20, minus $65, divided by 2, gives $116 countable. $994 minus $116 = $878 federal SSI.

**Break-even point.** With only wages and no other income, the federal SSI check reaches $0 at about (2 x FBR) + $85 per month. For 2026 that is about $2,073/month (our arithmetic: 2 x $994 + $85). Medicaid can continue past that point under Section 1619(b) (see related page).

**Student Earned Income Exclusion (SEIE).** For people under 22 who regularly attend school, college or job training. School thresholds: college 8 hours/week; grades 7-12 at least 12 hours/week; training 12 hours/week (15 with shop practice).

**PASS (Plan to Achieve Self-Support).** A written plan (form SSA-545) with a work goal and a timeline. Income and resources set aside under an approved PASS are not counted. PASS can let someone with SSDI also qualify for SSI. SSA has PASS specialists (via 1-800-772-1213).

**IRWE.** Costs for items or services a person needs to work because of their impairment, that they pay for themselves without reimbursement, and that are reasonable. Examples: some medications, attendant care, transport. Not allowed: health insurance premiums, or non-service animals.

**BWE (blind only).** Blind work expenses can include any work expense, such as taxes, meals at work, and transportation. They are subtracted after the one-half step, so they help more than IRWE.

**Deeming.** Some income of another person counts toward SSI when the person lives with an ineligible spouse, when a disabled child under 18 lives with parents, or when a noncitizen has a sponsor. Parental deeming stops at age 18. So many young adults see SSI rise at 18.

**Resources.** Countable resources must stay at or under $2,000 (individual) as of the first moment of each month. Excluded items include the home, one vehicle, household goods, burial funds up to $1,500, PASS funds, and ABLE funds up to $100,000.

**Reporting wages.** SSI recipients must report gross wages each month. Methods: my Social Security, the SSA Mobile Wage Reporting app, the automated phone line (1-866-772-0953), or the Payroll Information Exchange (PIE). With PIE, SSA gets wages directly from the employer (sign up with form SSA-8240). The person still needs to report until SSA confirms PIE is working.

**Overpayments.** SSI payments are based on income in the same month, so late reports create overpayments. If the person does not repay or respond within 30 days of the notice, SSA withholds 10% of the SSI payment by default. Asking for a waiver or appeal within 30 days pauses collection.

## Caveats and gotchas
- People often think one dollar earned costs one dollar of SSI. That is wrong for wages. It is about $1 lost for every $2 earned after $85.
- SSDI counts as **unearned** income for SSI. A person with both gets far less benefit from the $65 exclusion.
- The Wisconsin supplement rose 10% on 2026-05-01. It was the first change since 1994. Older materials still say $83.78. Wisconsin pays its own supplement, so it may be on a separate payment.
- SSA pages disagree on the wage reporting deadline: the wage page says "by the sixth day of the month," and the 2026-08-01 article says "the 10th." Tell people to report by the 6th. **(verify)** the regulatory deadline in POMS.
- SSI is paid on the 1st. When the 1st falls on a weekend or holiday, it is paid earlier. That is why the January 2027 increase arrives 2026-12-31. Two payments in one month do not mean an overpayment.
- Resources of an ineligible spouse or parent can be deemed too. This page does not give 2026 deeming allocation amounts. **(verify)** in POMS SI 01320 before using them.
- 2027 values are announced but take effect 2027-01-01. A tool must use the rate for the month being estimated.

## How ERI / a hackathon tool should use this
- An SSI estimator must apply exclusions in the order above. It must show "Estimate only, not a benefits determination" and route the user to WIPA at **1-877-826-1752**.
- The estimator must ask whether the person also gets SSDI or other unearned income, lives with a spouse or parents (deeming), and pays for any IRWE or BWE. If the answers are unknown, show a range, not one number.
- Include the Wisconsin supplement as a separate line. Show it as a state payment.
- Pick the FBR by month. Use $994 through December 2026 and $1,029 from January 2027.
- Push wage reporting. Offer reminders (6th of the month) and link to PIE and the SSA app. Never collect SSA login details.
- Do NOT tell anyone they "will lose SSI" or "will be overpaid." Do NOT give a final answer on deeming, PASS approval, or overpayment waivers. Route those to WIPA or a benefits specialist.
