# ABLE accounts

## TL;DR
- Since **2026-01-01**, a person qualifies for ABLE if blindness or disability **began before age 46** (it was 26 before).
- The 2026 annual contribution limit from all sources is **$20,000** (2025: $19,000). Working owners who do not contribute to certain employer retirement plans can add more ("ABLE to Work"), up to the one-person poverty level or their earnings, whichever is less.
- For SSI, the **first $100,000** in an ABLE account is not a countable resource. Over that, SSI is suspended (not ended), and Medicaid continues.
- **Wisconsin has no state ABLE plan yet.** 2023 Wisconsin Act 267 authorizes one, and DFI is working on it. Residents can open an account with any state's plan that accepts nonresidents.

## How it works
**Who can open one.** A person who is blind or disabled with onset before age 46. They either get SSI or SSDI on that basis, or self-certify with a doctor's diagnosis (per the ABLE Act's general rules) **(verify)** the certification rules with the chosen plan. One account per person.

**Money in.** Anyone can contribute: the owner, family, friends, or a trust. The total from all sources is $20,000 in 2026. A working owner can add more from their own earnings under ABLE to Work. Contributions are not SSI income to the owner, but the owner's wages are still counted as earned income when received.

**Money out.** Distributions for **qualified disability expenses** (QDEs) are not income for SSI and are not federally taxed. QDEs include education, housing, transport, employment training and support, assistive technology, health, and basic living expenses. For SSI:
- Housing distributions kept past the month they are received count as a resource.
- Funds held for a non-housing QDE stay excluded while waiting to be spent.

**SSI and Medicaid.** The first $100,000 is excluded. If the excess over $100,000 (alone) puts the person over $2,000, SSI is suspended but Medicaid continues, with no time limit. If **other** resources cause the excess, normal rules apply. After 12 months of suspension, SSI ends.

**Why it matters for work.** ABLE lets a worker on SSI or in 1619(b) save past the $2,000 limit. It also lets MAPP members save outside the $15,000 limit. It works well with ERI's employment goals: car repairs, training and work clothes are QDEs.

## Caveats and gotchas
- **Conflicting ABLE to Work figures.** ABLE NRC (2026 update) uses the prior-year poverty guideline ($15,650 continental). The SSA SSI spotlight lists $15,960. Another FAQ on the same SSA page says $19,000. ABLE NRC's ABLE to Work page shows an outdated "$19,000" annual limit and a total of $35,650. **(verify)** with the chosen plan or IRS guidance before quoting a number.
- Whether H.R. 1 (Public Law 119-21, 2025-07-04) changed ABLE rules, for example by making ABLE to Work, 529 rollovers or the Saver's Credit permanent, was not confirmed in sources we opened. **(verify)**
- Medicaid payback: on death, the state may claim remaining funds for Medicaid costs. That is the general ABLE rule, but we did not confirm Wisconsin specifics. **(verify)**
- Wisconsin's own plan may launch later. Recheck the DFI page at each review.
- Wisconsin tax treatment comes from ABLE NRC (2016 Wisconsin Act repealing the state plan and adding a subtraction). **(verify)** on the Wisconsin Department of Revenue ABLE page before advising.

## How ERI / a hackathon tool should use this
- A savings helper can show: "Up to $100,000 in ABLE does not count for SSI. Spend housing withdrawals in the same month." It must route the user to WIPA **1-877-826-1752** before large deposits or withdrawals.
- An eligibility check should ask "Did your disability begin before age 46?", not 26. Older content online still says 26.
- Do NOT recommend a specific state plan or investment. Link to ablenrc.org plan comparisons and to Wisconsin DFI.
- Do NOT treat ABLE to Work amounts as certain until the conflict above is resolved.
