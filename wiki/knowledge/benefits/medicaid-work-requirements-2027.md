# Medicaid work (community engagement) requirements starting 2027

## TL;DR
- Section 71119 of Public Law 119-21 (enacted **2025-07-04**) requires certain adults to show "community engagement" to get or keep Medicaid. States must start by **2027-01-01**. The HHS Secretary can grant good-faith exemptions, which must end by 2028-12-31.
- In Wisconsin it applies to **BadgerCare Plus adults 19-64** who are not pregnant and have no dependent child under 19 at home. That is about **200,000** people, and DHS's first estimate is that about **63,000** could lose coverage.
- How to meet it: **80 hours in a month** of work, volunteering, a work program or a mix; or at least half-time school; or **$580+ income in a month** (80 x federal minimum wage).
- **Not subject**: SSI Medicaid, MAPP, Family Care, IRIS, Partnership, people with an SSA disability determination, Medicare enrollees, and other listed exemptions. Wisconsin new applicants start **2027-01-01**. Current members start at renewals due **2027-03-31** and later.

## How it works
**Exemptions (federal list, used by Wisconsin).** Pregnant or postpartum. Medically frail or with special medical needs. Parent or caretaker of a child 13 or under or of a disabled person (Wisconsin also exempts parents of a child under 19 living with them). American Indian / Alaska Native. Veterans with a total disability rating. People meeting TANF/W-2 work rules, or in a FoodShare household and not exempt from FoodShare work rules. In SUD treatment. Incarcerated, or released within 3 months. Former foster youth. States cannot add exemptions.

**Medically frail (CMS IFC, 42 CFR 435.554).** Five categories:
1. Blind or disabled as defined in SSA section 1614.
2. Substance use disorder (unless in stable recovery for 5+ years).
3. Disabling mental disorder.
4. Physical, intellectual or developmental disability that significantly impairs one or more activities of daily living.
5. Serious or complex medical condition.

The condition must significantly impair the ability to comply. States must check data first (ex parte), using claims, encounter and HIE data. Self-attestation is allowed with limits until 2028-01-01. Medical frailty is reverified at least every 12 months.

**Short-term hardship (Wisconsin plans to allow).** Inpatient or similar care; a county under a disaster declaration; a county with high unemployment; extended travel for medical treatment.

**Verification in Wisconsin.** No monthly reporting. DHS checks at application and at annual renewal. It uses data first, and providers will not need to fill out DHS medical exemption forms. Non-exempt people who don't comply are denied or disenrolled after notice. They can reapply any time. People denied for this reason cannot get Marketplace premium tax credits.

## Caveats and gotchas
- **Most ERI participants are not subject.** People on SSI, MAPP, EBD Medicaid, Family Care or IRIS, or with an SSA disability determination, are outside BadgerCare Plus or exempt. The risk is disabled workers who are on **BadgerCare Plus** with no disability determination. Depending on their situation, a medical frailty exemption, an SSA disability determination or MAPP may be relevant. WIPA or Disability Rights Wisconsin can help a person sort out which, if any, applies.
- Homelessness and getting unemployment compensation are **not** exemptions in Wisconsin.
- The CMS fact sheet is inconsistent about caretakers: "under 14" in one place, "13 and under" in another (the same thing for whole years).
- Six-month eligibility renewals for expansion adults (another H.R. 1 provision) were not described on the Wisconsin pages we opened. **(verify)** whether and when they apply to Wisconsin's BadgerCare adults.
- An earlier DHS partner page listed the start as "December 31, 2026". The newer DHS member page and webinar say 2027-01-01 for applicants. Use 2027-01-01.
- The IFR comment period and any final rule could change details. **(verify)** at the next review.

## How ERI / a hackathon tool should use this
- A screener should first ask "What Medicaid do you have?" If the answer is SSI, MAPP, Family Care, IRIS or Medicare, show "This rule likely does not apply to you."
- For BadgerCare Plus adults: show the three ways to comply. Show that 80 hours of work, or $580 earned in one month, is enough. Show exemptions in plain language. ERI job placement can help people meet the rule.
- Remind people of DHS's general guidance: keep their address current, use an ACCESS account, and read DHS letters.
- Route the user to WIPA **1-877-826-1752** or Disability Rights Wisconsin for exemption questions.
- Do NOT say a person "will lose Medicaid". Do NOT decide medical frailty. DHS decides.
