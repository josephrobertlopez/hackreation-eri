# Wisconsin public workforce system (southern WI)

## TL;DR
- Wisconsin has **11 Workforce Development Areas**, each with a WIOA board. Five cover southern Wisconsin: **Southeastern WDB** (WDA 1), **Employ Milwaukee** (WDA 2), **WOW** (WDA 3), the **WDB of South Central Wisconsin** (WDA 10, which includes Dane) and the **Southwest WI WDB** (WDA 11, which includes Rock).
- The boards run local **Job Centers** (American Job Centers). DVR offices are often **co-located** in them, for example in Elkhorn, Kenosha, Janesville, Jefferson and Dodgeville. The statewide online system is **jobcenterofwisconsin.com**. To find a local center, call **888-258-9966**.
- **Apprenticeship** reached a record **18,524 registered apprentices** in 2025, and **about 11,400 youth apprentices** took part in 2024-25. Certified pre-apprenticeship had 1,121 completers.
- **Windows to Work** is a DOC-funded reentry employment program that **all 11 WDBs** deliver. In FY2023, 63.5% of released participants found jobs, at an average of $17.61/hour.

## How it works
- **WIOA Title I** pays for Adult, Dislocated Worker and Youth services through each board: career services, training scholarships (Southeastern Works lists manufacturing, health care, transportation, construction and IT) and supportive services. Employ Milwaukee also runs **Earn & Learn** (summer youth jobs) and a **reentry** program.
- **Job Centers** offer at least the core services in the Wisconsin Job Center Standards: resource rooms, job search help, employer recruitment help, links to W-2, FoodShare and BadgerCare, UI information and **vocational rehabilitation**. DVR is the WIOA Title IV partner.
- **Job Center of Wisconsin.** jobcenterofwisconsin.com is the job board, résumé and labor-market tool. wisconsinjobcenter.org hosts the directory and information pages.
- **Registered Apprenticeship** is overseen by DWD's Bureau of Apprenticeship Standards (BAS). **Youth Apprenticeship** is for high school juniors and seniors and is run through local consortia.
- **Windows to Work (W2W).** DOC contracts with each WDB. The pre-release curriculum starts 3-12 months before release and covers cognitive intervention, work skills, financial literacy, community resources and job seeking. After release, a W2W coach gives about 12 months of job search and retention help. It runs in 18 adult institutions and 5 county jails. In FY2023 it had 505 enrollments, and 193 of 304 released participants (63.5%) got jobs at $17.61/hour.
- **Other reentry resources:**
  - Ten DOC/DWD **institution-based Job Centers** plus one mobile center. The first opened at Oakhill CI in 2018.
  - Mobile CNC and welding labs.
  - **OARS**, a DOC/DHS program for people with serious mental illness. It starts 6-8 months before release and gives up to 2 years of community support.

## Caveats and gotchas
- The SWWDB website blocked automated reading. Its program list and Job Center addresses (Janesville, Beloit) came from the DVR directory and the DWD map, not from swwdb.org. **(verify)**
- WOW's page did not list Job Center street addresses. It points to wfdc.org. **(verify)** the Waukesha, West Bend and Ozaukee Job Center addresses.
- The WDBSCW homepage lists Windows to Work and Youth Apprenticeship but no Job Center addresses. **(verify)** the Dane County Job Center site. Madison DVR's Aberg Ave office is listed by DVR, not by the board.
- The Windows to Work outcome figures are **FY2023**. **(verify)** whether newer Becky Young reports exist.
- The BAS 2025 review has no disability-specific data. Its footer gives wisconsinapprenticeship.org, not apprenticeship.wi.gov. **(verify)** which URL is canonical.
- "Reentry" and "disability" overlap a great deal, but no opened source gives a count.

## How ERI / a hackathon tool should use this
- ERI's home board is **WDA 10 (WDBSCW)**, and ERI board members also sit on it. The best co-enrollment opportunities are WIOA training funds alongside DVR services, and Windows to Work for returning citizens with disabilities.
- A tool can show the **local Job Center and board by county** and should point waitlisted DVR consumers to them. It **must not** suggest that WIOA eligibility is the same as DVR eligibility.
- Treat apprenticeship and pre-apprenticeship as job-match targets that pay a wage. Check entry requirements with the program sponsor.
