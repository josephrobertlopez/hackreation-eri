# Wisconsin DVR structure and consumer process

## TL;DR
- The Division of Vocational Rehabilitation (DVR), part of the Department of Workforce Development (DWD), is Wisconsin's public VR agency. In FFY2025 it **served 33,687 people** and had **3,429 successful employment outcomes**, at an **average of $17.04/hour** and **24.89 hours/week**.
- DVR runs **11 Workforce Development Areas (WDAs)** with **35 field offices**. In southern Wisconsin these are WDA 1 (Kenosha/Racine/Walworth), WDA 2 (Milwaukee), WDA 3 (Waukesha/Ozaukee/Washington), WDA 10 (South Central, including Dane) and WDA 11 (Southwest, including Rock).
- An **Order of Selection waitlist took effect in December 2025**. On **2026-06-02** the Joint Committee on Finance approved more funding. DVR is releasing people from the waitlist Category 1 first and aims to end it by **2027-06-30**.
- DVR buys most job services from **contracted vendors** such as ERI, under the Statewide Service Fee Structure in effect **2026-07-01 to 2028-06-30**. Supported employment pays by milestone, for example Career Profile $1,000, systematic instruction $1,600/month and Transition to LTS $1,300.

## How it works
1. **Referral.** A person refers online, by phone at 800-442-3477, or on a paper form. Only basic information is needed. People who get SSI or SSDI because of their own disability are **presumed eligible**.
2. **Application.** DVR contacts the person to review and sign the application. A guardian signs for minors and for adults who have a guardian.
3. **Eligibility (60 days).** DVR gathers disability documentation and can buy assessments. An extension needs a specific agreed date. Staff start the extension request by day 45.
4. **Order of Selection.** An eligible person is put in **Category 1** (most significant disability limitations), **Category 2** (significant) or **Category 3** (all others). The rating looks at seven functional areas: mobility, communication, self-care, self-direction, interpersonal skills, work tolerance and work skills. While the waitlist is in place, people come off it by category and then by application date.
5. **IPE (90 days).** The counselor and the consumer write the Individualized Plan for Employment. It sets the job goal and lists every service. DVR cannot pay for a service that is not in the IPE.
6. **Services.** Services must be necessary, reasonable and appropriate. They include counseling, job development, supported or customized employment, IPS, training grants (FAFSA-based), assistive technology and benefits counseling. The counselor contacts the consumer at least every 30 days.
7. **Closure.** A case closes as successful after 90 days of stable employment. A consumer can ask to close at any time and can reapply later.

### Waitlist rules (Order of Selection, 2025-2027)
- People who already have an **approved IPE keep getting services**.
- A student who received documented Pre-ETS before the eligibility decision can **keep getting Pre-ETS** while on the waitlist. That covers job exploration, work-based learning, enrollment counseling, self-advocacy and workplace readiness. Job coaching, transportation and personal assistance need an IPE.
- **Adults on the waitlist get no DVR services**, and that includes Project SEARCH.
- A person at **immediate risk of losing a job** can ask the local office for specific services.
- The FAQ sends waitlisted people to the Job Center of Wisconsin at 888-258-9966 for other help.

### Vendor (service provider) model
- DVR counselors authorize services by purchase order to providers that have signed the **DVR Statewide Service Provider Agreement**. Providers deliver to the Technical Specifications and are paid by fixed fee or milestone.
- There are **nine statewide services**: Customized Employment, IPS, Internship/Temporary Work, Job & Task Analysis/Systematic Instruction, Job Preparation/Development/Hire/Retention, Student Work-Based Learning, Supported Employment, Vocational Evaluation, and Work Incentive Benefits Services. There are also "Other Services" such as OJT, Partners with Business, Skills to Pay the Bills and Walgreens REDI.
- **Supported Employment fees** (2026-2028): Career Profile $1,000; Job Prep & Development Plan $350; Job Development & Hire $1,600 standard, up to $2,200 preferred; Job & Task Analysis $275; monthly systematic instruction $1,600; retention without SI $500/month; Transition to LTS $1,300 within 6 months, otherwise $500. Hourly SI is $55.
- **Job Development fees**: Plan $550; Hire up to $1,900 with Rapid Hire; Retention $1,600 standard or $2,200 preferred, paid at 30, 60 and 90 days.
- **Supported employment limits**: the fee structure says "the maximum ongoing support DVR can pay for is 24 months for anyone age 25 or above, and 48 months for individuals under age 25," with more months possible based on individual need. Whether the 48 months is a lifetime cap is **(verify)**. For adults 25 and older, the IPE must name the LTS funder. Transition to LTS cannot happen before 90 days on the job.

## Caveats and gotchas
- **The waitlist changes month to month.** The FAQ says funding is approved and DVR is "working through the waitlist," but it does not say which categories are open. Do not tell anyone they will get services by a certain date. **(verify)** the current category status with DVR before publishing.
- **Provider waitlists are a separate thing from the OOS waitlist.** On 2026-08-31, 199 consumers statewide were waiting for a *provider*, and 160 of them were in **WDA 10 (Dane and the counties around it)**. Most were waiting for Supported Employment (85) or Job Development (73). That is ERI's home WDA, and the shortage of provider capacity there is a real opportunity.
- The FFY2025 report gives hours as 24.89. "24.9" is a rounded figure.
- The 2025-11-03 press release came before the waitlist and predicted that about 2,000 people would be affected right away. Do not present those figures as actual waitlist counts.
- WDA 11's Richland Center office had no main line in the directory.
- The fee schedule labeled "August 2026" is the current one. Pages in the archived `_archive/20260901-...` folder hold the 2024-2026 specs.

## How ERI / a hackathon tool should use this
- Treat DVR status as data: *not referred → referred → applied → eligible (category) → waitlisted or active → IPE → service → employed → closed*. A tool can show where someone is in that flow and the next step. It **must not** decide eligibility or category.
- Use the **60-day** and **90-day** clocks to prompt staff follow-up, for example "eligibility due by...".
- If someone is waitlisted, route them to Job Center services, WIPA benefits counseling, county or long-term-care funding, and Pre-ETS for students, instead of a dead end.
- Use the fee schedule for capacity planning only. Do not show billing amounts to consumers.
- Use the WDA 10 provider waitlist figure as evidence when ERI plans capacity.
