# Transportation to work in southern Wisconsin

## TL;DR
- **ADA paratransit costs $3-$4 per one-way ride** in the southern systems: Madison $3.25; Milwaukee, Waukesha, Racine and Kenosha $4.00; Janesville $3. Most systems need **next-day booking**. Approved personal care attendants ride free.
- **Fixed-route reduced fares** for riders with disabilities are $1.25 on MCTS and $1.00 on Madison Metro, with daily, weekly and monthly **fare caps**.
- **BRT.** Milwaukee's **CONNECT 1** runs on the Wisconsin Ave corridor. **CONNECT 2** (27th St) is still in planning. Madison's **Rapid Route A** (east-west) launched in **September 2024**. The north-south BRT is expected to open **about 2028**.
- **Outside paratransit areas,** counties use **s. 85.21** WisDOT aid for elderly and disabled transportation. Examples are Waukesha County's shared-fare taxi and RideLine, and Dane County Human Services rides. These programs have limited hours and need booking ahead, so the commute often decides whether a job match works.

## How it works
- **ADA paratransit eligibility depends on function, not diagnosis.** The question is whether the person can board, ride and exit a bus on their own, or get to a stop. Madison requires a written application **and an in-person assessment** covering gait and balance, route recognition, and memory. A decision comes within 21 days, people can appeal within 60 days, and eligibility is recertified every 3 years. Eligibility can be **conditional**, for example only in snow or ice. Racine, Kenosha and Waukesha require a physician-signed form.
- **Payment is changing.** Transit Plus riders moved to the **WisGo card or Umo app on 2026-01-05**. Paper tickets were last sold 2026-02-27 and remain valid through 2026. Agency-paid rides for long-term care members move to WisGo later in 2026.
- **Agency fares.** Madison is holding a hearing on **2026-10-28** on raising paratransit *agency* fares and contract rates for the Commute Card and Unlimited Ride Pass programs. The $3.25 rider fare is not proposed to change.
- **s. 85.21 county aid.** WisDOT funds counties to transport older adults and people with disabilities. Every county gets at least 0.5% of the appropriation, and the rest is split by each county's share of elderly and disabled residents. Counties may contract with nonprofit or for-profit operators.
  - *Waukesha County ADRC*: shared-fare taxi for people 60+ or with a disability designation (ADRC pays $9 per eligible one-way ride from 2026-01-01); RideLine for wheelchair users; Eras volunteer rides.
  - *Dane County*: Human Services rides (608-242-6489), RSVP driver escort, Stoughton Transit, and **YW Transit** for low-income work trips (608-257-1436).
- **Long-term care.** Family Care and IRIS plans can pay for work transportation. Janesville says LTC members can get passes through their social worker.

## Caveats and gotchas
- **"Paratransit takes as long as the bus."** MCTS says outright that a van trip can take as long as the same trip by bus. Shift start times have to allow for pickup windows.
- **Weekend and late shifts** often fall outside county programs and outside Kenosha's reduced Saturday and Sunday paratransit hours.
- MCTS's opened pages did not give CONNECT 1's exact opening date. A February 2024 post called it "recently launched." **(verify)**
- No opened page gave a launch date for **CONNECT 2**. **(verify)**
- Madison's BRT FAQ links "Route B" to the north-south corridor upgrade. Treat 2028 as a projection.
- The Janesville paratransit service area was not stated. **(verify)**
- The Waukesha Metrolift document is dated **November 2023**. **(verify)** the fares and hours.
- The DVR waitlist means waitlisted consumers **cannot get DVR-funded transportation**.

## How ERI / a hackathon tool should use this
- A job-match tool should **score commute feasibility**, not just distance: bus or BRT route and frequency at the shift times, whether the address is inside paratransit or the 3/4-mile buffer, county program hours, and the transfer count. A good job with an impossible commute is not a match.
- Show fares as ranges and link to the operator. Do not quote exact fares in ways that will go stale.
- A tool **must not** determine paratransit eligibility. It can explain the process and link to the application.
