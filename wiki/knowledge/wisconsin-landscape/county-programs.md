# County disability programs relevant to employment (southern WI)

## TL;DR
- In Wisconsin, **counties are the access point** for CCS (mental health and substance use recovery), CLTS (children's waiver), the ADRC (which handles adult long-term care enrollment and runs the **disability benefit specialists for ages 18-59**), and much of specialized transportation.
- **CCS** is a Medicaid psychosocial rehabilitation benefit for **all ages**. It can include **job-related skills training**. Dane County launched it in **2015**, and it needs no county match. ERI is a Dane County CCS provider.
- **Milwaukee County DHHS** has a **Disabilities Services Division (DSD)**, the **ADRC of Milwaukee County** (1230 W. Cherry St, 414-289-6874) under Aging & Disabilities Services, and the **Office for Persons with Disabilities** (1220 W. Vliet St). The ADRC lists **Youth Transition** and **Benefits Specialists**.
- Other southern counties run these functions through their **human services departments and ADRCs** (phone list in [long-term-care-system.md](long-term-care-system.md)).

## How it works
- **CCS.** A county intake worker checks eligibility, meaning unmet needs that could lead to hospitalization in a crisis. The member works with a recovery team to choose services from certified providers. Dane County's 2015 launch drew more than 25 provider agencies. DVR's supported employment guidance names county mental health programs (CSP, CCS) as possible LTS resources and allows a county case worker on the team.
- **CLTS.** The county human services agency coordinates services. In Dane County, case management is contracted out. For example, **Avenues to Community** (Fitchburg) does CLTS support and service coordination under a Dane County DHS contract. ERI's site lists "CLTS: Youth & Family Services."
- **ADRC youth transition** helps students move from children's to adult long-term care. That handoff usually happens around 17.5-18, when adult Family Care or IRIS eligibility is set.
- **Benefit specialists versus WIPA.** ADRC disability benefit specialists help people apply for and keep benefits. Work-incentives planning for SSI and SSDI beneficiaries is WIPA's job (1-877-826-1752). ERI is a WIPA provider.

## Caveats and gotchas
- **Milwaukee County naming.** The DSD page still says "Disabilities Services Division." The ADRC brochure header says "Aging & Disabilities Services." **(verify)** the current org chart, and the name and intake path for the Behavioral Health Services/CCS unit. The BHS page could not be opened in this pass.
- **Dane County youth transition programs** beyond the ADRC's youth transition service were not confirmed. **(verify)**
- "**CBS benefits specialists**" in the original brief: no Dane County program by that name was found. The confirmed service is the ADRC's **Disability Benefit Specialist** program (ages 18-59). **(verify)** whether "CBS" meant something else.
- The DHS CCS page did not mention Medicaid or the DHS 36 rule. Dane County's 2015 release calls CCS a Medicaid benefit.
- Each county's phone menu differs. Use the ADRC number as the front door.

## How ERI / a hackathon tool should use this
- Build a **county resolver**: an address leads to the county ADRC, Job Center, DVR office, WDB and transit options.
- In intake, ask whether the person is in CCS or CLTS. If yes, the county team should help coordinate the employment plan, with the person's consent.
- A tool **must not** screen for CCS or CLTS eligibility. Send the person to county intake.
