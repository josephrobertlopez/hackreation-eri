# Rehabilitation Act Sections 501, 503 and 504

## TL;DR
- **Section 501:** federal agencies must not discriminate and must take affirmative action. Agencies set goals of **12%** employees with disabilities and **2%** with targeted disabilities in two grade clusters.
- **Section 503:** federal contractors must not discriminate and must take affirmative action. **A final rule published 2026-08-21, effective 2026-09-21, rescinded the 7% utilization goal, the utilization analysis, and the requirement to invite applicants and employees to self-identify (Form CC-305).** Non-discrimination, accommodation, outreach and the written affirmative action program (50+ employees and $50,000+ contract) remain.
- **Section 504:** recipients of federal financial assistance, and federal programs, must not discriminate on the basis of disability. Each funding agency enforces it.

## How it works
**Section 501 (federal government as employer).** Federal agencies are not covered by ADA Title I. Section 501 does the same job for them, and adds affirmative action. Agencies report progress against the 12% and 2% goals in their annual plans. Federal applicants with disabilities can also be hired noncompetitively under [Schedule A](hiring-incentives-wotc-schedule-a.md). Federal employees use their agency's EEO process. They must contact an EEO counselor within 45 days, not file with the EEOC directly.

**Section 503 (federal contractors).** Contractors and subcontractors above the dollar threshold must include an equal-opportunity clause, not discriminate, provide reasonable accommodation, and do outreach and recruitment. Larger contractors keep a written affirmative action program. Before 2026-09-21, contractors also had to invite applicants and employees to self-identify using Form CC-305, and measure themselves against a 7% goal. DOL's 2026 rule removed both. DOL said the disability inquiry requirement conflicted with ADA limits on asking about disability. Contractors are "no longer required to consider quantitative data" on individuals with disabilities when judging outreach.

**Section 504 (federal funding).** Any program that takes federal money, such as many schools, hospitals, universities, workforce boards, and nonprofits with federal grants, must not exclude or discriminate against qualified people with disabilities. That includes employment. For employment complaints, courts apply ADA Title I standards to 504 claims **(verify: 29 U.S.C. 794(d); not opened)**.

## Caveats and gotchas
- **The 7% goal and CC-305 are outdated material.** Many training decks, vendor pitches and older wiki pages still cite them. As of 2026-09-21 they are rescinded. The eCFR page we opened on 2026-10-23 still displayed the old 60-741.45 text, which probably means eCFR had not yet been updated **(verify)** that eCFR now reflects the rule.
- Contractors may still *choose* to invite voluntary self-identification under other authority. Whether that is allowed, and in what form, is not settled here **(verify)**.
- The DOL ODEP fact page still says "$10,000" for Section 503. It is out of date; the regulatory threshold was $15,000 and is now $20,000.
- Litigation, or further rulemaking, could change Section 503 again. Recheck before 2027-01-15.
- We could not open 29 CFR 1614.203 itself; the 12%/2% figures come from a federal agency plan that cites it **(verify)**.

## How ERI / a hackathon tool should use this
- Business-services outreach to federal contractors should **stop leading with "help you meet your 7% goal."** Lead with outreach obligations, accommodation and talent pipeline value, which still apply.
- A tool must NOT ask job seekers to fill out CC-305-style self-ID forms "for the employer." Disclosure is the job seeker's choice; see [disclosure](disclosure-and-accommodation-requests.md).
- Tag employer records with "federal contractor? (Y/N/unknown)" and "receives federal funds? (Y/N/unknown)" so staff know which laws may apply. Do not auto-determine coverage.
- For federal job seekers, link to the Schedule A page.
