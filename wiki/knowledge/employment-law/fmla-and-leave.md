# FMLA, Wisconsin FMLA and leave as an accommodation

## TL;DR
- **Federal FMLA:** employers with **50+ employees**. Eligible workers have **12 months** of service, **1,250 hours** in the past 12 months, and work where the employer has 50 employees within 75 miles. They get **12 workweeks** of unpaid, job-protected leave per 12 months (26 for military caregiver leave).
- **Wisconsin FMLA (Wis. Stat. 103.10):** employers with **50+ permanent employees**. Eligible workers have **52 consecutive weeks** and **1,000 hours**. Leave is smaller: **6 weeks** for birth or adoption, **2 weeks** to care for a family member, **2 weeks** for one's own serious health condition, per **calendar year** (8 weeks family leave max).
- The laws **run at the same time**, and the worker gets the more favorable term. Wisconsin complaints go to the ERD within **30 days**.
- **Leave can also be an ADA or WFEA reasonable accommodation**, even when FMLA is used up or doesn't apply, unless it causes undue hardship.

## Key facts
| Item | Federal FMLA | Wisconsin FMLA |
|---|---|---|
| Employer size | 50+ employees in 20+ workweeks (current or prior year); public agencies and schools any size | 50+ permanent employees (DWD: in at least 6 of last 12 months) |
| Service required | 12 months | More than 52 consecutive weeks |
| Hours required | 1,250 in prior 12 months | 1,000 in preceding 52 weeks |
| Worksite rule | 50 employees within 75 miles | None stated |
| Own serious health condition | Within 12 weeks total | 2 weeks |
| Care for family member | Within 12 weeks total (child, parent, spouse) | 2 weeks (child, spouse, domestic partner, parent) |
| Birth / adoption | Within 12 weeks total | 6 weeks, starting within 16 weeks of birth or placement |
| Total cap | 12 weeks (26 military caregiver) | 8 weeks family leave; plus 2 weeks medical |
| Period | 12-month period (employer chooses method) | Calendar year (per DWD) |
| Pay | Unpaid | Unpaid; employee may substitute paid or unpaid employer leave |
| Health insurance | Continued on same terms | Continued on same conditions |
| Complaint | DOL WHD, 1-866-487-9243 | ERD within **30 days** of the action or of learning of it |
| Citation | DOL WHD FMLA page | Wis. Stat. 103.10(1)(c), (2)(c), (3), (4), (5); DWD |

## How it works
**Overlap.** Many Wisconsin workers qualify under both laws. The leave runs concurrently. For each question, apply whichever law is more generous. Once federal leave goes past the Wisconsin amount, only federal terms apply to the rest (DWD). An employer policy or union contract that is more generous applies over both.

**Who gets only one.** Someone with 1,000 but not 1,250 hours may qualify for Wisconsin leave only. Someone at a site with few nearby employees may qualify for Wisconsin leave only, since Wisconsin has no 75-mile rule. Many part-time ERI job seekers fall in these gaps; check both.

**Intermittent leave.** Federal FMLA allows intermittent or reduced-schedule leave when medically necessary. Bonding leave is intermittent only if the employer agrees. Wisconsin leave can be taken in blocks or intermittently (DWD).

**Wisconsin enforcement and remedies.** ERD handles complaints. Remedies include back pay, reinstatement, interest, and attorney fees and costs. Liquidated damages are not available. Other damages may be possible in a separate civil action after the administrative process ends (DWD).

**Leave as a reasonable accommodation.** Under the ADA (EEOC guidance; Wisconsin's WFEA likely treats leave similarly **(verify)**):
- Leave can be an accommodation, including leave beyond FMLA, or for someone who isn't FMLA-eligible.
- Accrued paid leave is used first, then unpaid leave.
- The employer generally must hold the job open unless that would be an undue hardship.
- Penalizing someone for leave taken as an accommodation is retaliation.
- The ADA and FMLA are analyzed separately. Meeting one doesn't satisfy the other.

## Caveats and gotchas
- **Wisconsin's leave is shorter but easier to qualify for.** Don't assume "not FMLA-eligible" means "no leave rights."
- The **30-day Wisconsin deadline is very short**. It is far shorter than the 300-day deadline for discrimination complaints.
- The federal FMLA complaint deadline (generally 2 years, 3 if willful) was not on the page we opened **(verify)** at dol.gov.
- Wisconsin also has a Bone Marrow and Organ Donation Leave law for 50+ employers (mentioned by DWD; not detailed here).
- "Serious health condition" has detailed definitions in both laws that we did not reproduce **(verify)** before applying them to a case.
- Wisconsin has no general state paid family leave program as of 2026-10-23 **(verify)**.

## How ERI / a hackathon tool should use this
- A leave screener can ask: employer size, months worked, hours in the last year, and reason. It can then show "may be eligible for federal FMLA / Wisconsin FMLA / neither," and always add "leave may also be available as a disability accommodation."
- Show the **30-day Wisconsin deadline** prominently whenever someone reports a leave denial or firing after leave.
- A tool must NOT tell someone they are or aren't entitled to leave. Route to the WI ERD (608-266-6860), DOL WHD (1-866-487-9243), Disability Rights Wisconsin, or an attorney.
- Job coaches should help workers document leave requests in writing; see [disclosure and accommodation requests](disclosure-and-accommodation-requests.md).
