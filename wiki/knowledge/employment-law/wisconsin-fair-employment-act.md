# Wisconsin Fair Employment Act (disability, arrest and conviction record)

## TL;DR
- The WFEA applies to **any employer with at least one employee**, plus the state. It covers many workers the ADA misses, since the ADA starts at 15 employees.
- Wisconsin's disability definition is its own: an impairment that **"makes achievement unusually difficult or limits the capacity to work,"** a record of one, or being perceived as having one.
- **Arrest and conviction records are protected classes.** An employer may act on a conviction or pending charge only if its circumstances **"substantially relate"** to the circumstances of the particular job.
- File with the **DWD Equal Rights Division (ERD) within 300 days**. Remedies are back pay (up to 2 years before filing), reinstatement, interest and attorney fees. There are **no emotional-distress or punitive damages**.

## How it works
**Disability.** The test asks whether the impairment makes achievement unusually difficult or limits the capacity to work. DWD notes that alcoholism and drug addiction are disabilities under state law, and that AIDS is covered. A work injury can be a disability. Employers may still apply the same performance standards to everyone and ban drugs and alcohol at work.

**Reasonable accommodation and hardship.** Employers must reasonably accommodate unless that would be a **hardship** on the business. DWD describes hardship as difficult or expensive relative to the employer's size and resources, overly disruptive, or a fundamental change to the business. The worker usually has to ask unless the need is obvious. The worker is entitled to an effective accommodation, not necessarily their preferred one.

**Safety.** An employer may deny a job for safety reasons only with objective or medical evidence of significant risk, and only after considering accommodation, per DWD.

**Arrest and conviction records.**
- Employers generally may **not ask about arrests**, except pending charges.
- They **may ask about pending charges and convictions** if they make clear they will consider them only if substantially related to the job.
- **No blanket bans.** Each job and record must be assessed individually.
- "Substantially related" means some overlap between the circumstances of the offense and the circumstances of the job. DWD's examples: theft and a cashier job are related. Drunk driving is related to truck driving, but probably not to cashiering.
- Coworker or customer preference is not a valid reason. Neither is how upsetting the crime is.
- An employer may refuse to hire someone who **lied** on an application. The reason is dishonesty, not the record. DWD advises answering honestly and offering to explain.
- Licensing agencies have extra duties, including written reasons, a chance to show rehabilitation, and a 30-day determination on request (Wis. Stat. 111.335(4)).

**Complaint process at ERD.**
1. File Form ERD-4206 online (dwd.wisconsin.gov/er/complaints/) or by mail or fax.
2. An Equal Rights Officer investigates. The employer must answer in writing. Mediation and settlement are available at any time.
3. Initial determination: **Probable Cause** (goes to hearing) or **No Probable Cause** (dismissed unless the complainant appeals within the period stated in the determination).
4. Hearing before an Administrative Law Judge. Evidence given to the investigator is not carried over, so everything must be presented again. A lawyer is advisable but not required.
5. Appeals go to the Labor and Industry Review Commission (LIRC), then to circuit court.

**Remedies.** Back pay, reinstatement, lost benefits, interest, attorney fees and costs, cease-and-desist orders and training. Front pay may replace reinstatement in some retaliation cases. **Not available:** compensatory damages for emotional harm, punitive damages, liquidated damages, jury trials, class actions.

## Caveats and gotchas
- **Association is not covered.** Discrimination because of a family member's disability is not covered by state law, per DWD. Use the ADA (15+ employees) for that.
- **Medium confidence on disability text.** We could not open Wis. Stat. 111.34 (the disability section) directly; the site blocked automated access. The accommodation and hardship description comes from DWD ERD pages. Read 111.34 before quoting statutory language **(verify)**.
- We could not open **Wis. Stat. 111.395** to confirm its subject **(verify)**.
- **When the 300 days starts.** DWD pages describe it as 300 days from the action "or from when you became aware of it." The statute says 300 days after the discrimination "occurred." Use the earlier date to be safe **(verify)** with ERD for specific facts.
- Remedies are narrower than federal law. Someone at a 15+ employer may also have ADA rights, including damages; this is a reason to talk with a lawyer about where to file.
- The NPC appeal deadline is short and is stated in the determination letter. Read it the day it arrives.

## How ERI / a hackathon tool should use this
- Use the WFEA as the **default framework for small Wisconsin employers** (1-14 employees).
- Job-matching for people with records: let the job seeker choose whether to record offense types, so a tool can *flag possible "substantially related" questions for a counselor*. A tool must **NOT** decide that a record disqualifies someone, auto-exclude jobs, or tell an employer an offense is "related."
- Offer job seekers a plain-language explainer: "In Wisconsin, employers generally can't ask about arrests, and can only consider convictions related to the job."
- Encourage honest application answers. Never coach someone to hide a conviction.
- Route disputes to ERD (608-266-6860 / 414-227-4384), Disability Rights Wisconsin, or an attorney. Legal Action of Wisconsin or similar may help with record issues **(verify)** current programs.
