# Wisconsin Employment First (2017 Wisconsin Act 178)

## TL;DR
- **2017 Wisconsin Act 178** (2017 Assembly Bill 625; enacted 2018-03-28, published 2018-03-29) created **Wis. Stat. 47.05, "Employment First initiative."**
- Publicly funded programs serving **working-age** people with disabilities (16+, or the transition-services age) must **prioritize competitive integrated employment (CIE)**.
- DWD leads, with DHS and DPI. They must keep a **joint plan with performance improvement targets** (updated at least every two years) and each publish an **annual progress report**.
- The current plan (SFY 2026-27, revised January 2026) and the SFY 2026 report (revised June 2026) **set no numeric CIE targets**. A workgroup is developing an analysis to set them.

## How it works
**What the law requires.** Act 178 is a policy and accountability law. It does not create a new right for an individual or ban any service. It tells state agencies to:
1. prioritize CIE when supporting working-age people with disabilities in activities outside the home;
2. collaborate under DWD's lead;
3. write a joint plan with specific performance improvement targets and coordination methods;
4. report at least yearly on the plan's targets, the number of people served and the number in CIE, percentage change, average hours and wages, barriers, and regional or county breakdowns where data exist; and
5. publish the plan and reports.

**What it does not do.** It does not end subminimum wage or prevocational services. Rules under it may not limit access to or choice of services.

**The 2026-27 plan.** Three cross-agency targets: raise awareness that work is possible, align state and local services, and prepare students through K-12 career pathways and work-based learning. Agency activities include:
- **DVR:** raise median hourly wages; equitable service for underrepresented groups; more measurable skills gains.
- **DHS:** publish SFY 2020-2025 CIE data; CIE training; update IRIS service definitions for the 2026 waiver renewal; Career Planning and Discovery in CLTS; raise IPS fidelity; new IPS sites each year.
- **DPI:** Transition Improvement Grant; Wisconsin Community on Transition; Youth Apprenticeship and career pathways for students with IEPs.

**The SFY 2026 report.** It covers activity from 2025-07-01 to 2026-06-30. Most outcome data is from July 2023 to June 2025. DVR's CIE-eligible count rose from 22,689 (SFY 2023) to 27,865 (SFY 2025). DVR reported a measurable skills gains rate of 48.1% as of 2026-04-24, against 67.6% for SFY 2025.

## Caveats and gotchas
- **Act number confirmed:** 2017 Wisconsin Act 178. Some sources say "Act 178 of 2018" because it was enacted in 2018.
- **No numeric targets yet.** Do not tell funders that "Wisconsin's CIE goal is X%."
- **Counts are not comparable across agencies.** People can be in more than one system. The report flags the IPS figure as possibly duplicative and says DPI's count fell because of stricter wage-matching.
- DVR wage figures are a mix of medians and averages from different dates. Quote them with their date.
- The DVR waitlist (from December 2025) affects how many people DVR can serve during this plan; see [WIOA and VR](wioa-title-iv-and-vr.md).

## How ERI / a hackathon tool should use this
- Report ERI outcomes in **Act 178 terms**: CIE yes/no, hours per week, hourly wage, and county. That lets ERI data line up with state reporting.
- A dashboard can benchmark ERI placements against the state's published medians ($15.00/hr, 20 hrs for DVR), with the source date shown.
- A tool must NOT present Act 178 as giving an individual a legal right to a job or a particular service. Route service disputes to the Client Assistance Program (Disability Rights Wisconsin) or the relevant agency.
