# Disability disclosure and accommodation requests

## TL;DR
- **Disclosure is the person's choice.** There is no legal duty to disclose. But the employer generally has no duty to accommodate until it knows an accommodation is needed. Most people disclose **when they need an accommodation** (JAN).
- A request can be **plain English, oral or written, at any time**. It does not need the words "ADA." It can come from a **family member, health professional or job coach** (EEOC).
- **Disclose before problems pile up.** Employers do **not** have to rescind discipline or bad reviews that came before the request (JAN).
- **JAN** (askjan.org, 800-526-7234) gives free, confidential accommodation advice to workers, employers and job coaches.

## Key facts
| Item | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Form of request | Plain English; oral is enough; no magic words | EEOC 2002 guidance |
| Who can ask | The person, or a family member, friend, health professional or other representative | EEOC 2002; EEOC intellectual disabilities guide |
| When | Any time during application or employment | EEOC 2002; JAN Q&A |
| Must the worker ask even if the employer knows? | Generally yes | JAN disclosure article |
| Employer must start on its own only if | It knows of the disability, knows or should know it's causing problems, and knows or should know the disability prevents a request | JAN disclosure article |
| Documentation | Only enough to show the disability and the need; not complete records | EEOC 2002; JAN Q&A |
| No documentation | When disability and need are obvious or already documented | EEOC 2002 |
| Pre-offer, disability obvious or disclosed | Employer may ask whether an accommodation is needed and what type | EEOC intellectual disabilities guide |
| Third parties at interviews | Employer may not ask a job coach or relative anything it couldn't ask the applicant | EEOC intellectual disabilities guide |
| Past discipline | Need not be rescinded | JAN disclosure article |
| Standards | Same performance standards may apply; lowering a standard is not an accommodation | JAN Q&A |
| Response time | No fixed deadline; respond as quickly as possible; unnecessary delay can violate ADA | JAN Q&A; EEOC 2002 |
| Confidentiality | Disclosed medical information must be kept confidential and separate | 29 CFR 1630.14; EEOC intellectual disabilities guide |

## How it works
**Deciding whether and when to disclose.** JAN says the right timing depends on why you are disclosing. Common points:
- *On the application or before an offer:* only if an accommodation is needed for the hiring process, such as an interpreter, extra time on a test, or an accessible format. The employer must accommodate the application process even if it doubts it can accommodate the job.
- *After an offer, before starting:* a common time to arrange start-day accommodations.
- *On the job, when a need arises:* the most common time. JAN advises telling the employer "as soon as possible."
- *When performance or conduct problems are already happening:* JAN's guidance is that the person can explain that the problems are related to a disability and ask for an accommodation. Disclosure does not erase past discipline. For a specific situation, route to JAN, Disability Rights Wisconsin or an attorney.

**What to disclose.** Not just a diagnosis. Explain **how the condition affects the job** and **what would help** (JAN). Usually a diagnosis is not needed in the first request.

**To whom.** A supervisor or HR. If the employer has a written accommodation procedure, follow it too. Telling HR about a condition without linking it to a need does not by itself start the process (JAN).

**How to request: sample language.** Adapted from JAN's letter elements: date, names and addresses, the request, how the condition affects work, suggested accommodations, an invitation to discuss, a requested response time, and a thank-you.

> I am writing to request an accommodation for a medical condition. Because of my condition, [describe effect on work, e.g., "standing for long periods causes increased pain"]. I believe the following could help me do my job: [option 1], [option 2]. I'm open to other ideas. I can provide more information if needed. Could we talk about this in the next week? Thank you.

A written request is optional, but it documents what was asked and when (JAN). JAN suggests keeping a copy.

**What employers can ask.**
- *Pre-offer:* "Can you perform these job functions, with or without accommodation?" and "Show us how." No questions about diagnosis, severity, medications, or leave history.
- *After a request:* reasonable documentation of the disability and the need, if not obvious.
- *During employment:* disability-related questions only if job-related and consistent with business necessity, for example after objective evidence that a condition is affecting performance or safety.

**Job coaches.** EEOC lists a job coach as a possible accommodation. A coach can also help the employer and worker figure out what accommodation is needed. A call from a parent to a supervisor about job problems was treated as a request in an EEOC example.

**Wisconsin.** DWD likewise says the person usually must ask unless the need is obvious, and points employers to JAN.

## Caveats and gotchas
- The EEOC accommodation guidance dates from 2002 and predates the ADAAA. Its process guidance is still the standard reference, but check eeoc.gov for newer documents **(verify)**.
- JAN is technical assistance, not a lawyer. It cannot tell someone whether they have a claim.
- "Disclose" to a VR counselor or ERI job coach is different from disclosing to the employer. The job seeker must consent before ERI shares disability information with an employer **(verify)** against ERI's release-of-information policy and DVR rules.
- Employers may choose among effective accommodations. The worker's preference gets primary consideration but is not guaranteed.

## How ERI / a hackathon tool should use this
- Build a **disclosure planner**: the job seeker picks when (application / offer / on the job / not now), what to share (functional effects, not diagnosis), and who (supervisor / HR / job coach). Save drafts privately.
- Generate a **draft request letter** from the JAN elements, with a "this is not legal advice" note and a JAN link.
- A tool must NOT disclose disability information to an employer without the job seeker's explicit, recorded consent, and must NOT require a diagnosis to generate a letter.
- If a request is denied or ignored, show routes: JAN (800-526-7234), Disability Rights Wisconsin, EEOC (1-800-669-4000), WI ERD (608-266-6860), or an attorney.
