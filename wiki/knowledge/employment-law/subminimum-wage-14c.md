# Subminimum wage and FLSA Section 14(c)

## TL;DR
- Section 14(c) of the Fair Labor Standards Act lets DOL certify employers to pay workers with disabilities a **productivity-based wage below the federal minimum ($7.25/hour)**.
- DOL proposed phasing out 14(c) on **2024-12-04** (89 FR 96466). **DOL withdrew that proposal effective 2025-07-07** (90 FR 29817), saying it lacked statutory authority. **14(c) remains legal federally.**
- Nationally, as of **2025-10-01**, **642** employers held certificates and reported **35,534** workers paid subminimum wages, down from about 424,000 workers in 2001.
- In Wisconsin, an employer needs **both** a DWD special minimum wage license (DWD 272.09) and a federal 14(c) certificate. A Wisconsin-only certificate count was not available from the sources we opened **(verify)**.

## How it works
**The certificate.** DOL issues a 14(c) certificate when it is needed to prevent loss of employment opportunities. The worker's pay is set by productivity on the task, compared with experienced workers without disabilities doing similar work nearby, and reviewed periodically.

**Wisconsin's added layer.** Wisconsin's own minimum wage law also applies. To pay below it, a rehabilitation facility or sheltered workshop needs a DWD special minimum wage license. The employer must explain the license terms to each worker, and to a parent or guardian if appropriate, both orally and in writing. Workers can ask for review within 60 days, through the ERD.

**Section 511 interaction.** Since 2016-07-22, youth 24 and younger cannot start subminimum wage work without DVR documentation of transition services, a VR attempt, and career counseling. Everyone paid subminimum wage must get career counseling and referral from DVR every 6 months in the first year, then once a year. See [WIOA and VR](wioa-title-iv-and-vr.md).

**Why DOL withdrew the rule.** DOL read the statute's "shall" as requiring it to issue certificates where needed. It said states ending 14(c) do not change federal duties. It said that falling use does not prove there is no need, and noted commenters' worries about job and service loss. Any new attempt would need a new proposed rule.

**Employment First** policy points the other way: public systems should prioritize CIE. See [Wisconsin Employment First](wisconsin-employment-first.md). Wisconsin's Act 178 plans and reports do not mention 14(c).

## Caveats and gotchas
- **Do not say "14(c) is being phased out."** That was true only as a proposal from 2024-12-04 to 2025-07-07. Federal law has not changed. Congress could still act, and some states have ended subminimum wage under state law **(verify)** current list.
- **Wisconsin count unknown.** WHD's certificate list is a Tableau dashboard filterable by state, which we could not read. Download the crosstab for Wisconsin before citing a number **(verify)**.
- The DWD license page lists license terms that ran through 2018-03-31 and may be stale. Check DWD for current licensees **(verify)**.
- One DPI survey in the Act 178 report found 1% (23) of employed former students with IEPs working in a sheltered workshop or community rehabilitation program. That is not a 14(c) count.

## How ERI / a hackathon tool should use this
- A job-matching tool should treat **subminimum-wage jobs as outside the CIE target**, and must not recommend them.
- For people currently earning subminimum wage, a tool can flag "eligible for career counseling and CIE exploration." It must not pressure anyone or judge their current choice.
- Don't present 14(c) policy as settled. Show "last checked" dates.
- Route wage questions to DOL WHD (1-866-487-9243) or the WI ERD, and route individual situations to Disability Rights Wisconsin.
