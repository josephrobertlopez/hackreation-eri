# Hiring incentives - WOTC, Schedule A, and accessibility tax benefits

## TL;DR
- **WOTC lapsed.** It covers only workers who **began work on or before 2025-12-31**. It was **not** extended in 2025 legislation, per IRS pages updated in 2026. Wisconsin DWD still accepts applications for 2026 hires and holds them as "pending" in case Congress reauthorizes it retroactively.
- When active, WOTC gives up to **$2,400** for SSI recipients and **VR referrals, including Ticket to Work Employment Network referrals like ERI's**, and up to **$9,600** for some disabled veterans. **Form 8850 must be filed within 28 days of the start date.**
- **Schedule A** (5 CFR 213.3102(u)) lets federal agencies hire people with intellectual, severe physical or psychiatric disabilities **noncompetitively**, with a "Schedule A letter" from a doctor, licensed VR specialist or disability-benefits agency.
- Accessibility tax help for employers: the **Disabled Access Credit** (Form 8826, small businesses) and an **architectural barrier removal deduction of up to $15,000 a year** (any size).

## How it works
**WOTC (when authorized).** The employer and applicant complete Form 8850 by the day of the job offer. The employer submits it, with ETA 9061, to the state workforce agency within 28 days of the start date. Wisconsin's deadline has "no exceptions." The state certifies whether the worker belongs to a target group. The employer then claims the credit on its tax return. The credit is limited to income tax liability, or for tax-exempt employers, Social Security tax (veterans only). Target groups include TANF, veterans, ex-felons, designated community residents, VR referrals, summer youth, SNAP, SSI, long-term family assistance, and long-term unemployed.

**Why the VR referral group matters to ERI.** People referred by DVR, or by an Employment Network under Ticket to Work, can qualify as VR referrals. SSI recipients qualify separately. ERI can help employers screen, but the employer files.

**Schedule A.** A federal agency may appoint a person with an intellectual disability, severe physical disability, or psychiatric disability without competition. The person gives proof of disability before hiring. It can come from a licensed medical professional, a licensed state or private VR specialist, or any federal, state, D.C. or territorial agency that issues disability benefits. OPM's examples of severe physical disabilities include blindness, deafness, paralysis, missing limbs, epilepsy and dwarfism. After hiring, the person or HR completes SF-256. After two years of satisfactory service the person may convert to permanent status. A DVR counselor may be able to write a Schedule A letter, since a state VR agency is an accepted source; whether a given counselor counts as a "licensed VR specialist" is a question for DVR **(verify)**.

**Wisconsin state jobs.** Wis. Stat. 230.25(1n) lets an agency request expanded certification, adding up to 3 names of persons with a disability, when it is hiring persons with a disability. Disabled veterans have separate certification and noncompetitive appointment rules (230.25(1g), 230.275).

**Disabled Access Credit and barrier deduction.** A small business can claim the nonrefundable Disabled Access Credit each year it spends on eligible access expenditures, which are listed in the Form 8826 instructions (commonly cited examples include interpreters, readers and accessible equipment **(verify)**). Any business can deduct up to $15,000 a year for removing architectural and transportation barriers. Both can be used in the same year. The deduction is then total spending minus the credit.

## Caveats and gotchas
- **Do not assume WOTC was extended in 2025. We found no evidence of that.** IRS pages updated 2026-03-20 and 2026-07-20 still say hires after 2025-12-31 do not qualify. Washington state's 2026-07-20 notice says no reauthorization had been confirmed. Check congress.gov and irs.gov before telling employers anything about 2026 hires **(verify)** that no reauthorization passed between July and October 2026.
- If Congress reauthorizes WOTC retroactively, only applications filed on time (28 days) may count, so employers may want to keep filing. That is DWD's practice, not a guarantee.
- DAC dollar limits: the common figures ($250 minimum, $10,250 maximum, $5,000 maximum credit; IRC 44) did not appear on the IRS pages we opened **(verify)** against the Form 8826 instructions. The deduction section number (IRC 190) also did not appear on the page **(verify)**.
- Schedule A text: we could not open 5 CFR 213.3102(u) on eCFR. Details come from OPM's page **(verify)**. Federal hiring practices changed in 2025; check agency announcements **(verify)**.
- Wisconsin's 230.25(1n) item text was incomplete on the page we opened, and any DPM disability hiring program under it was not opened **(verify)**.

## How ERI / a hackathon tool should use this
- Do not market "$2,400 per hire" to employers for 2026 hires without a reauthorization caveat. Show WOTC as "lapsed; may be reauthorized; file within 28 days to preserve it."
- A tool can generate a WOTC reminder for employers: "Form 8850 due within 28 days of [start date]," counted in calendar days.
- A tool must NOT tell a job seeker's SSI status to an employer without consent. Screening for WOTC reveals target-group membership.
- For federal applicants, offer a Schedule A explainer and "ask your DVR counselor for a Schedule A letter."
- Tax questions go to the employer's tax adviser. ERI does not give tax advice.
