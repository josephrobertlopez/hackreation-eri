# ADA Title I (employment)

## TL;DR
- Title I covers private employers, state and local governments, employment agencies and unions with **15 or more employees** (each working day in 20+ calendar weeks this year or last). Smaller Wisconsin employers are still covered by the [Wisconsin Fair Employment Act](wisconsin-fair-employment-act.md).
- A "qualified individual" can do the **essential functions** of the job **with or without reasonable accommodation**. The employer must accommodate unless that causes **undue hardship** (significant difficulty or expense).
- Medical questions follow a three-stage rule: **none before a job offer**, broad exams **after a conditional offer** (if everyone in the job category gets them), and only **job-related and consistent with business necessity** questions during employment.
- File a charge with the EEOC within **300 days** in Wisconsin (180 days is the base rule; Wisconsin has a state agency, so 300 applies). Filing with either the EEOC or the WI Equal Rights Division is dual-filed with the other.

## How it works
**Who is protected.** A person has a disability if an impairment substantially limits a major life activity, if they have a record of one, or if the employer treats them as having one. Since the ADA Amendments Act (effective 2009-01-01), "substantially limits" is read broadly and is "not meant to be a demanding standard." Ignore medication, hearing aids and other mitigating measures when deciding.

**Qualified individual.** The person must meet the job's skill, education and experience requirements and be able to do the essential functions, with or without accommodation. Marginal duties do not count. A job description written before the job was posted is evidence, not the final word.

**Reasonable accommodation.** This is any change to the application process, the work environment, or how the job is done. Examples: job restructuring, modified schedules, equipment, interpreters, leave, and reassignment to a vacant job as a last resort. The employer may pick among effective options and may pick the cheaper one. It cannot force an accommodation on someone who does not want it. It cannot lower pay to cover the cost.

**The interactive process.** A request does not need the words "ADA" or "reasonable accommodation." Plain English is enough, and it can be oral. A family member, friend, health professional or job coach can make it. The request starts an informal dialogue. Ignoring a request or delaying without reason can itself violate the ADA. See [disclosure and accommodation requests](disclosure-and-accommodation-requests.md).

**Documentation.** If the disability or need is not obvious, the employer may ask for documentation limited to showing the disability and the need. It generally may not ask for complete medical records.

**Undue hardship.** Judged case by case against the employer's resources. Cost is measured net of tax credits and outside funding, such as DVR-purchased equipment. Unduly disruptive accommodations, or ones that would fundamentally change the business, can also qualify.

**Medical questions, in three stages.**
1. *Before an offer:* no questions about whether the applicant has a disability or how severe it is, and no medical exams. Asking "Can you do X, with or without accommodation?" is fine.
2. *After a conditional offer, before work starts:* exams and questions are allowed if everyone entering that job category gets them. Withdrawing the offer based on results must be job-related and consistent with business necessity.
3. *During employment:* only job-related and consistent with business necessity. Voluntary wellness programs have their own rules.

**Confidentiality.** Medical information goes on separate forms in a separate medical file. It may be shared only with supervisors (restrictions and accommodations only), first aid and safety staff (if emergency treatment may be needed), and government investigators.

**Retaliation.** It is illegal to punish someone for opposing discrimination, filing a charge, or helping an investigation. It is also illegal to coerce, threaten or interfere with someone using ADA rights, including requesting accommodation.

**Filing.** Charges go through the EEOC Public Portal, by phone intake, in person, by mail, or through the WI Equal Rights Division. Under worksharing agreements, a charge filed with the state agency is dual-filed with the EEOC. Weekends and holidays count toward the deadline, but a deadline falling on one rolls to the next business day. Internal grievances do not stop the clock.

## Caveats and gotchas
- **Use 300 days, but file early.** Ongoing harassment runs from the last incident. Each separate act, such as a firing or a denied promotion, has its own clock.
- **The 2002 accommodation guidance predates the ADAAA.** Its accommodation process content still applies, but its disability-definition discussion is outdated. Use 29 CFR 1630.2 for the definition.
- **Associational discrimination** (being treated badly because of a family member's disability) is covered by the ADA but not by Wisconsin law, per DWD. That matters for workers at employers with 15+ employees.
- **Current illegal drug use** is not protected. People in recovery are.
- Damages caps for ADA compensatory and punitive damages (42 U.S.C. 1981a) scale with employer size **(verify)**; we did not open that source.
- EEOC guidance is not binding law, and federal agency guidance priorities shifted in 2025. Check eeoc.gov for current documents before quoting guidance **(verify)**.

## How ERI / a hackathon tool should use this
- Treat "15+ employees?" as a routing question. Under 15 → Wisconsin law only (ERD). 15+ → both (dual filing).
- Show a **deadline calculator** that counts 300 calendar days from the event date, labeled "estimate." It must tell users to contact the EEOC or ERD promptly and never say they are "out of time."
- Job-matching and intake tools must **not ask employers to collect diagnosis or medical history pre-offer**. Frame screening questions as "Can you perform [function], with or without accommodation?"
- Store any medical or accommodation information **separately** from the general job-seeker profile, with access limited by role.
- A tool must NOT tell someone whether they "have a disability under the ADA" or whether they have a case. Route them to Disability Rights Wisconsin, the EEOC (1-800-669-4000), the WI ERD (608-266-6860), or an attorney.
