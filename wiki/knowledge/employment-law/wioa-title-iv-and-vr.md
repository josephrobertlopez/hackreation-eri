# WIOA Title IV, vocational rehabilitation and Wisconsin DVR

## TL;DR
- Title IV of WIOA (signed 2014-07-22) amended the Rehabilitation Act. It funds state VR agencies, which in Wisconsin is **DWD's Division of Vocational Rehabilitation (DVR)**. The goal is **competitive integrated employment (CIE)**: at least minimum wage, the customary wage, benefits, integration, and advancement.
- **Pre-Employment Transition Services (Pre-ETS)** in Wisconsin serve students with disabilities **ages 14-21** (until the 22nd birthday). Students can get Pre-ETS as "potentially eligible" without a full VR application.
- **Section 511** bars 14(c) employers from paying youth **age 24 or younger** subminimum wage unless DVR documents Pre-ETS or transition services, a VR attempt, and career counseling.
- **Wisconsin DVR has been on an Order of Selection waitlist since December 2025.** On 2026-06-02 the Joint Committee on Finance approved more funding, and DVR's goal is to end the waitlist by **2027-06-30**.

## How it works
**The VR path.** A person applies to DVR. DVR determines eligibility, assigns an Order of Selection category, and, when the category is open, develops an Individualized Plan for Employment (IPE) with the person. DVR purchases services from vendors like ERI, such as job development, job coaching and supported employment. The case closes after a stable employment outcome.

**Order of Selection (OOS).** When a state VR agency cannot serve everyone, it must serve people with the most significant disabilities first. Wisconsin uses three categories:
- Category 1: most significant disability limitations.
- Category 2: significant limitations.
- Category 3: all other eligible people.

Categories are based on functional limits in seven areas: mobility, communication, self-care, self-direction, interpersonal skills, work tolerance, and work skills. Under the waitlist, new eligible applicants wait. **People who already have an approved IPE keep their services.**

**Pre-ETS during the waitlist.** Students who got documented Pre-ETS *before* their eligibility determination can keep getting Pre-ETS while waitlisted: the five required activities. They cannot get IPE-level services like job coaching, transportation or personal assistance until their category opens. Project SEARCH gets limited services. Adults on the waitlist get no DVR services.

**Potentially eligible students.** A student who meets the student definition and has never been found eligible or ineligible can get Pre-ETS through the PE Request form (DVR-18207-E). School staff can verify disability. A student who needs more than Pre-ETS must apply. DVR advises high school students to refer themselves about two years before leaving school.

**Section 511 (subminimum wage for youth).** Before a 14(c) employer can pay someone 24 or younger less than minimum wage, DVR must document:
1. Pre-ETS or IDEA transition services, and
2. a VR application that ended in ineligibility, or a closed case after a reasonable try with supports (up to 24 months for supported employment goals), and
3. career counseling and information and referral, which may not promote subminimum wage work.

Wisconsin DVR says a student or youth "may not work for subminimum wage without first applying for DVR services." See [subminimum wage 14(c)](subminimum-wage-14c.md).

## Caveats and gotchas
- **Ages differ by purpose.** Pre-ETS: 14-21 in Wisconsin. Section 511 "youth": 14-24. The working-age definition in Wisconsin Employment First: 16+.
- **Waitlist status changes.** The FAQ did not say which categories are currently open. Check DVR before telling anyone how long they will wait **(verify)** current category status.
- The 15% Pre-ETS reservation of federal VR funds (34 CFR 361.65) and the full text of 34 CFR 361.36 (OOS) and 361.48 were not opened on 2026-10-23 **(verify)**.
- Pre-ETS money stops on the student's 22nd birthday or when they leave school, whichever is first.
- People who disagree with a DVR decision can contact the **Client Assistance Program (CAP)**, run by Disability Rights Wisconsin.

## How ERI / a hackathon tool should use this
- A **CIE check** in a job-matching tool can test the five CIE elements: wage ≥ minimum and customary, benefits, integrated setting, advancement. Flag, don't block, roles that fail one element, and send them to a counselor.
- An intake tool can show "You may be able to get Pre-ETS now" for students 14-21, and "DVR has a waitlist; here is what you can still do" for adults.
- A tool must NOT predict someone's OOS category or wait time, or tell anyone they are ineligible. DVR decides. Route disagreements to CAP (Disability Rights Wisconsin).
- A tool must NOT match youth 24 or under to subminimum-wage jobs.
