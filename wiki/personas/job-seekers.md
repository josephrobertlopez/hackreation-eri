# Job seeker personas

## 1. Marcus: the average user (primary persona)
> "I want a real job, like 20 hours. But my mom says if I make too much I lose my SSI."

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Age | 23 |
| Where | Sherman Park, Milwaukee; rides MCTS buses |
| Disability | Autism (the 2nd most common primary disability at DVR); ADHD |
| Benefits | SSI (federal rate up to $994/month in 2026, $1,029 in 2027; living arrangements can lower it) and Medicaid |
| Programs | DVR (has an IPE); possibly referred to ERI for Supported Employment and benefits counseling |
| Education | High school diploma + a transition program |
| Work history | School-based work experience at a grocery store; a summer job stocking shelves |
| Wants | 20-25 h/week, daytime, stocking, warehouse sorting or kitchen prep (DVR's top job types); about $15-17/h |
| Phone | Android, prepaid data plan; uses YouTube and TikTok, rarely email |
| Reads | Short, plain text; long paragraphs and forms overwhelm him |

**Goals**
- A job he can get to by bus in under 45 minutes
- Clear steps: what to do next, by when
- Money of his own without "messing up" SSI

**Fears and pain points**
- Losing SSI or Medicaid (the #1 fear across ERI's stories)
- Interviews; unclear instructions; noisy, chaotic workplaces
- Job sites with long applications, CAPTCHAs, timed personality tests
- Not knowing who to call: DVR counselor, ERI specialist, or benefits person

**Accommodations that help:** written step-by-step instructions, a quiet space, a job coach for the first weeks, a predictable schedule.

**Design implications**
- Plain language at a 6th-grade level, one action per screen, big tap targets
- Show the bus and the commute time up front
- A benefits "heads-up," never benefits advice: "Talk to a benefits specialist before you say yes"
- Let him share a job with his specialist or his mom in one tap

## 2. Dana: working with mental health conditions
> "I can do 15 to 20 hours if the schedule is steady. More than that and I crash."

41, Madison. Depression, anxiety, PTSD; on **SSDI** (inspired by the kind of path in [Susan's story](https://eri-wi.org/susans-story/)). In a recovery program (CCS). Has a college background but wants low-stress work. She has stitched together two or three part-time jobs. Wants a budget that shows SSDI plus wages month by month. Fears using up Trial Work Period months without knowing it.
**Design for:** calm UI, no urgency language, and a "what does this job do to my month" view.

## 3. Renee: the professional returner
> "I was scared to death to leave the security of disability." (paraphrasing the theme of [Sarah's story](https://eri-wi.org/sarahs-story/))

38, Middleton. Epilepsy, now controlled; on **SSDI** and using **Ticket to Work** (ERI PLUS). She has a strong résumé and a career gap. Employers have been hesitant. Needs: flexible schedule, no driving requirement, and help reporting wages to SSA correctly. Very tech-comfortable; uses a laptop and LinkedIn.
**Design for:** filters for "no driver's license required" and "flexible," plus a wage-reporting checklist.

## 4. Jordan (16) and his mom Lisa: turning 18
16, Green Bay. Intellectual disability; on CLTS; IEP at school. Lisa is the main user. She's juggling guardianship vs supported decision-making, SSI at 18, DVR referral, and school-to-work. Uses Facebook groups and ERI's [Before Age 18](https://beforeage18.org) site.
**Design for:** a timeline by age, a checklist, and "print for the IEP meeting."

## 5. Family member / support person (secondary)
Wants visibility without taking over. Often the one reading benefits letters. Needs a "shared with me" view and plain explanations.

## 6. Hiring manager at a Milwaukee employer (secondary)
Runs a warehouse or kitchen and has open shifts. Unsure what accommodations cost (often nothing) and worried about "getting it wrong." Doesn't know ERI exists. Wants pre-screened candidates, one contact person, and job-coach support.
**Design for:** a 2-minute "post a job with ERI" flow, plus an inclusive job-post checker.
