# ERI staff personas

ERI has about 29 staff ([public staff page](https://eri-wi.org/staff/)). The most common roles are **employment specialists** and **benefits specialists**, spread across Madison, Milwaukee, Green Bay, and north and northeast Wisconsin. Workload details below are **assumptions to confirm with ERI**.

## 1. Alex: the average employee (employment specialist)
> "I spend more time writing reports than I'd like. If I miss a deadline, we don't get paid for work I already did."

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Role | Employment Specialist (Supported Employment, DVR fee-for-service) |
| Where | Milwaukee area; works from home, the car, and client job sites |
| Background | 3-7 years in human services; bachelor's in psychology, criminology, or rehab; CESP certification likely |
| Caseload | About 15-25 active clients (assumption), at every stage: career profile → plan → job development → hire → monthly support |
| Tools (assumed) | Laptop, phone, email, calendar, a case-notes system, Word/PDF report templates, spreadsheets, DVR portal or email for reports |
| Travel | Lots: client homes, employers, job sites, bus rides with clients |

**A typical week**
- Meets clients to build career profiles and job development plans
- Cold-calls and visits employers; searches job boards for each client
- Coaches at job sites; checks in with supervisors
- Writes a report after almost every milestone, **due within 5 business days** (per DVR rules)
- Coordinates with DVR counselors, long-term care managers, families and benefits specialists

**Goals**
- Get the *right* job for each client, not just any job
- Keep clients employed past 90 days
- Spend less time on paperwork and searching listings

**Pain points**
- Matching is manual: job leads are scattered across emails, job boards and memory
- Report deadlines across many clients; documentation has to show specific required elements
- Not sure if every milestone got billed, since billing may be done by someone else
- Rural and transit barriers make good matches hard

**Tech comfort:** medium. Happy with simple web tools; hates double entry and new logins.

**Design implications**
- A caseload view: "3 reports due this week," "2 new matches for Marcus"
- A copy-ready summary for reports and for clients
- Works on a phone in a parking lot
- No new logins; export to what they already use

## 2. Pat: benefits specialist (WIBS / CWIC)
Deep expert in SSI/SSDI/Medicaid rules. Does intake, collects signed releases (ROI), verifies benefits with SSA, and writes individualized benefits reports. Mostly remote and statewide. Pain: chasing signatures and documents; re-explaining the same rules. Wants tools that bring clients in **better prepared**, but will reject anything that gives wrong benefits math.

## 3. Finance director
Owns revenue (program fees were about 55% of FY2025 revenue). Wants every authorized and delivered service billed correctly, and nothing billed that wasn't. Comfortable with spreadsheets and exports; thinks in exception reports and dollars at stake. May come from a healthcare billing background (an assumption, not a description of ERI's actual finance staff).

## 4. Client services director / program manager
Oversees employment and benefits teams. Cares about quality, compliance, deadlines and staff workload. Wants a dashboard of late reports, missing documentation and outcomes.

## 5. Web developer (the maintainer)
ERI's senior web developer has 20+ years building sites and third-party tools (public bio). WordPress multisite. Will inherit your code. Wants: standard stack, no paid lock-in, a README that runs, escaped output, no secrets in code.
