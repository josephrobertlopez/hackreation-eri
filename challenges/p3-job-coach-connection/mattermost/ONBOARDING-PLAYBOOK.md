# Playbook: onboarding a client onto the Ally chat

**Status: draft for ERI coach review.** Written for the hackathon prototype with fictional people. It is a starting point, not ERI policy. Anything about consent, privacy, or records must be checked by ERI before real use.

**Who it is for:** a job coach getting one client started.
**Time:** about 30 minutes for the first session, then short check-ins.
**Goal:** the client knows who Ally is, chose to use it, can reach it on their own phone, and knows how to reach a person.

## The rules that come first
1. **Consent before any AI.** The DVR provider agreement says any AI tool used with a DVR consumer's information needs that person's prior written or verbal consent. No consent, no Ally. The client can say no, and can change their mind later.
2. **Say clearly who Ally is.** Ally is an AI, not a person and not the coach. The client should be able to say that back to you.
3. **Collect as little as possible.** Job tasks and how the client likes to be spoken to. No diagnoses, medicine, case notes, benefit amounts, or home address in the chat or in Ally's instructions.
4. **The coach stays in charge.** The coach can read the chat, correct Ally, and take over.
5. **Emergencies are never for Ally.** The client must know: call 911, or tell the person in charge.

## Before the session (coach, 15 minutes)
- [ ] Task guide is ready: a few tasks, short steps, one sentence each (see the P2 task template).
- [ ] The coach has reviewed any safety wording in the guide. Ally must not invent steps.
- [ ] The client's private channel exists and has only the client, the coach, and Ally in it.
- [ ] The employer is **not** in the private channel.
- [ ] Plain-language consent script is printed or ready to read (below).
- [ ] The client's phone or a shared device is available, with the app or Safari page ready.
- [ ] You know the client's preferred way to get information: words, pictures, audio, or a mix.

## Session 1: step by step
### 1. Welcome (3 minutes)
Say it in your own words. A starting point:
> "This is a helper you can ask when you are stuck at work. It is a computer program, not a person. I can read everything you write to it."

### 2. Consent conversation (5 to 10 minutes)
Go slowly. Check understanding. Use pictures or paper if that helps. Cover:
- What Ally does: answers questions about your tasks, one step at a time.
- What Ally is: an AI. It can be wrong. It never replaces me.
- Who can see the chat: you, me, and Ally. Not your employer.
- What Ally will not do: help with emergencies, pay, benefits, or health. It will point to me instead.
- You can say no. You can stop any time. Saying no does not change your support.

Then ask: "Do you want to try it?" Record the answer in whatever way ERI requires (written or verbal). **The prototype has no consent record. Production needs one.**

### 3. Get connected (5 minutes)
- [ ] Sign in on the client's phone. Use the client's own account, not a shared one.
- [ ] Open the private channel together.
- [ ] Add the page to the Home Screen if using Safari, so it opens like an app.
- [ ] Turn on notifications only if using the Mattermost app (not available in Safari).

### 4. Set up the phone's own tools (5 minutes)
Ask what already helps the client, and turn on what they want. Do not push all of them.
- [ ] Dictation (mic key on the keyboard) to talk instead of type.
- [ ] Speak Screen or VoiceOver to hear replies read aloud.
- [ ] Larger Text, Bold Text, or higher contrast.
- [ ] Reduce Motion.
- [ ] Guided Access if the client wants the phone locked to one app.

### 5. Practice together (5 minutes)
Use a safe, easy question the client already knows the answer to:
- Client types or says: `@ally what is step 1 of stock the shelves?`
- Read Ally's answer together. Ask: "Did that make sense?"
- Try one question Ally cannot answer, so the client sees "I am not sure. The coach can help."
- Show how to send a photo, with a printed fake page first.
- Show the emergency rule again: call 911 or tell the person in charge.

### 6. Close (2 minutes)
- Ask the client to say back, in their own words: Who is Ally? Who can see the chat? What do I do in an emergency?
- Agree how and when the client should message you, without promising an instant reply.

## After session 1
| When | What the coach does |
| --- | --- |
| Same day | Read the first messages. Fix anything Ally got wrong in the guide. |
| Day 2 to 3 | Short check-in: is it helping, is it confusing, do you want it on? |
| End of week 1 | Read the chat history for wrong or unhelpful answers. Update the guide. Ask the client if they want to continue. |
| Every month | Repeat the consent question. Confirm the client still wants Ally. |

## Turning Ally off or leaving
- The client can stop at any time. Remove Ally from the client's channel and keep the coach channel.
- Decide with ERI what happens to chat history. The prototype has no retention policy.

## Signs it is not working (stop and change something)
- The client cannot say who Ally is.
- The client uses Ally for emergencies, health, pay, or benefits instead of a person.
- Ally gives a wrong or unsafe answer more than once. Fix the guide or turn Ally off.
- The client seems upset by it, or is using it because they feel they must.
- The employer asks to see the client's chat. The answer is no unless the client chooses to share.

## What the prototype does not have yet
- A recorded consent step.
- Real logins and protected transport.
- A retention and deletion policy.
- A way for a client to review or delete their chat history.
- ERI's own AI policy and coach review of the answers.
